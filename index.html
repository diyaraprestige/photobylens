How a lens is rendered

1

Your photo stays yours

Decoded and rendered in your browser. Every lens makes a new copy, so the original file is never altered or cropped.

2

AI reads and retouches

Your own free Gemini finds the subject, maps the depth and decides the edit: focus point, aperture, white balance, exposure, highlights, shadows and colour.

3

Optics, not filters

Blur grows with distance from your focus point, scaled by focal length and aperture. Lights bloom into true bokeh: cat's-eye, swirl, soap-bubble or anamorphic oval.

4

Each lens's character

Micro-contrast, colour, vignette, distortion, fringing, glow, flare and grain are tuned to each lens's well-known rendering.

A phone sees roughly a 26mm field of view. Zoom and macro lenses crop in to match their framing, always keeping your subject whole; wide and fisheye lenses keep your full frame and add their projection. Nothing is redrawn or invented: it is your real photo, retouched and rendered through the lens. Results are faithful renderings of each lens's style, not captures from the real glass.
